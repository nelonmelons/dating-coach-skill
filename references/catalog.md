# Technique Catalogue (full method, deduplicated)

Scope: male-to-female dating in a Chinese context (WeChat, Moments, dating apps, street/social approaches, dates). Every distinct principle, technique, diagnostic, signal and script from the source notes, grouped by stage. Chinese terms are kept where the source uses one.

Evidence/ethics tags:
- **[research-backed]**: consistent with relationship/persuasion research.
- **[plausible/untested]**: sensible, no direct evidence either way.
- **[contradicted by research]**: the evidence points the other way; use the correction given.
- **[MANIPULATIVE → honest version: …]**: depends on lying, faked persona, pressure, ignoring refusals, alcohol or evading a block. Do not coach the original. Coach the honest version, or "do not use".

Coach rule that overrides everything below: a "no", a pull-back, a block or a deletion is information, not a test. Tease the teasing; answer real questions honestly.

## 1. Mindset & identity

- **Attracted, not chased (女生不是追来的，是吸引来的)**: put effort into building yourself, not into pursuing one woman. A man who can list her virtues but none of his own is "a salesman with no product". [plausible/untested]
- **The monitor and the machine (显示器与主机)**: words only display the "machine" (character, worldview, lifestyle). No script makes someone like you; she works out who you are over many messages. Fix the life first, then the lines. [research-backed: perceived traits, not lines, drive attraction]
- **Neediness is the master variable (需求感)**: nearly every failure broadcasts "I need this to work": instant replies, long texts, double-texting, explaining, agreeing with everything, repeated invites after a decline. [research-backed in moderation: desperation and low self-respect are unattractive. But showing that you like her is attractive (reciprocal liking), so cut neediness, not warmth]
- **Emotional stability = no neediness (情绪稳定 = 没有需求感)**: don't over-read her reply times, posts or likes. Don't screenshot her posts asking friends "what does this mean?". "If she replied, she replied; if not, fine." [plausible/untested]
- **Three don'ts (不怕、不给、不要)**: don't show fear, don't over-give, don't need. [plausible/untested]
- **Self first (自我优先 / 自尊自重)**: when your needs clash with her requests, yours come first. If you're exhausted you don't drive across town at midnight: "I'm wiped tonight, let's do Thursday." [research-backed for self-respect and boundaries. Don't turn it into deliberate unavailability]
- **Project first, girl second (项目第一，女生第二)**: have a life you'd live anyway and invite her into it, rather than building plans around her. [plausible/untested]
- **Incidental investment (顺便)**: frame time and money as things you'd spend anyway ("I'm getting Thai food anyway, come along") so you aren't attached to a return. [plausible/untested]
- **Don't try to move her (不要感动她)**: "Being moved isn't liking (感动不是喜欢)." Grand devotion to someone who isn't into you produces guilt and obligation, and then you must keep raising the stakes. [research-backed: unrequested favours create indebtedness, not liking]
- **Five types of men women dislike**: assumes kindness wins her; thinks she's different from all other women; sacrifices himself to please her; treats her as a goddess; confesses deeply to move her. [plausible/untested]
- **Rejection is selection**: "If she has an excuse (work, exams), she didn't pick you. That's all." A woman who's keen works around obstacles. Don't argue with an excuse. [plausible/untested. Sometimes the excuse is real, so allow one honest reschedule]
- **Reverse paradox (接受不被喜欢你才自信)**: accept that some people won't like you, and you become confident, so more people do. [plausible/untested]
- **Abundance cures neediness**: talking to more people makes you calmer with each one. [plausible/untested. Honest version: be truthful that you're dating around if asked. His "10+ chats a day → cold 'sage mode' → they chase" is MANIPULATIVE → honest version: meet more people so no single outcome matters, without performing coldness]
- **Enjoy without owning (享受使用权，不要在意拥有权)**: early on, don't act possessive. She may be seeing others. [plausible/untested. Drop the "usage rights" framing of people]
- **No stated agenda early**: don't announce marriage or any purpose on contact; "any purpose puts pressure on her." Many men are sincere; few don't pressure her. [plausible/untested]
- **Mindset beats scripts**: creepiness is cured by changing how you think about the interaction (normal social contact between equals), not by learning lines. "Accidents aren't scary; panicked reactions to accidents are (意外不可怕，应激行为才可怕)." [plausible/untested]
- **Prepared improvisation (预案库)**: "all spontaneous reactions were designed in advance." Rehearse stories, answers to common questions, and topic branches before dates. [research-backed: rehearsal reduces anxiety. MANIPULATIVE → honest version: rehearse true stories only, not invented biographies]
- **Ten-year view (long-term vs short-term)**: short-term interest can be bought with money, looks or a persona. Long-term tests your core. Faked traits collapse like a product that doesn't match its listing photo and gets returned. Basis for long-term: authenticity, personality, and "a character that gives hope" (potential). [research-backed]
- **Potential stock (潜力股)**: being poor matters less than showing no trajectory. Compare yourself with yesterday, read, train, practise speaking. Don't complain. [plausible/untested]
- **Grit and luck**: big outcomes are partly luck, but you only catch it if you stay in the game. "What matters is resilience (心力), not ability." [plausible/untested]
- **Circle (圈子)**: your peer group affects your trajectory and how you're perceived. Spend time with people who push you up. [research-backed. Drop his "distance yourself from poor parents"]
- **Don't change for her; change for yourself**: attract the people who like the real you rather than becoming a shapeless "四不像". [research-backed]
- **Exchange mindset (交换思维)**: relationships, work and mentors all run on mutual value. People who want results without investing (in fitness, skills, earning) "want to freeload". [plausible/untested]
- **Simp origins (舔狗)**: over-pleasing often comes from upbringing: conditional love, denial-based parenting, "obedient = good". Notice it, don't blame parents, rebuild a sense of worthiness (配得感). [research-backed: contingent self-worth and anxious attachment]
- **Ordinary confidence vs resentment (普信男 redefined)**: what repels isn't being ordinary and confident, it's bitterness: calling women materialistic, dismissing others' success as luck or money. [research-backed: hostility is unattractive]
- **Multi-factor attraction (反二极管思维)**: attraction isn't just looks and money. Humour, a rich life, stability, cooking, risk-taking and EQ all count. Different women weight these differently (看人下菜碟). [research-backed]
- **Status over correctness (不管对错，只看强弱)**: claim that being right matters less than staying composed. [contradicted by research as a relationship rule (defensiveness and contempt predict breakups). Keep only: stay calm instead of arguing about trivia]
- **Make her a bit afraid of you (畏惧之心 / 位置)**: the claim that a woman who "doesn't dare touch your phone" respects you. [contradicted by research; fear-based deference marks coercive relationships. Do not use]
- **Women are all X (慕强, "7-day memory", "99% mood-driven", "no slow-warming women, only ones who don't like you")**: essentialist generalisations. [contradicted by research. Treat them as heuristics about individuals at most. "No slow-warmers" is partly useful: a long stall with no warmth usually means no]

## 2. Presentation & online profile

- **Display surface (展示面)**: clothes, photos and WeChat Moments are your first gate. If the surface fails, chat can't save you. [research-backed: first impressions from photos are strong]
- **Look expensive, not handsome (显贵)**: fabric quality > brand > style. Plain, well-fitting black and white beats loud logos and patterns. [plausible/untested]
- **Three parts face, seven parts styling (三分长相七分打扮)**: "overall handsome" means styling, posture, speech, gaze and hair, not just features. [research-backed: grooming and style move ratings substantially]
- **Perfume**: a good fragrance is the cheapest quality-of-life signal. [plausible/untested]
- **Grooming and body tells**: nose hair, shrunken clothes, fidgeting, leg-shaking and swaying read as nervous. Fewer movements read as more presence. Hold eye contact. [research-backed]
- **Body**: some muscle, not extreme. [plausible/untested]
- **Moments content**: real activities (sport, cooking, travel, friends, work) with positive energy. No sad quotes, complaints or rants. [plausible/untested]
- **Rebuild before re-contact (朋友圈重建)**: before re-approaching someone who went cold, refresh avatar and display name, delete old cringe posts, and post for 6–7 days about things you really do. [plausible/untested. MANIPULATIVE if it fabricates a life → honest version: post only genuine activities. Never rebuild a profile to evade a block or deletion]
- **Montage method (蒙太奇展示法)**: stretching one trip into months of posts. [MANIPULATIVE → honest version: post real life at its real pace; the mismatch costs more when you meet]
- **Stock or reused photos as "proof" (meal, dog, wine, bank VIP lounge, "just played tennis")**: [MANIPULATIVE → honest version: send a real photo of what you are doing, or nothing. Do not use]
- **Hard value vs soft value (硬价值 / 软价值)**: hard value (money, looks, body, status) sets which level of partner you can match and gets you "on the field". Soft value decides whether you win. More hard value gives you more room for error. [plausible/untested]
- **Soft-value list**: [research-backed for humour, confidence, kindness/stability, passion. Others plausible/untested]
  - *Pre-selection (预选)*: other women like or value you (Matthew effect). Means being socially valued, not chasing many.
  - *Career passion (事业)*: work you care about, even modest ("a kindergarten teacher who loves it").
  - *Circle (圈子)*: quality friends. Don't showcase low-class or hostile friends. A friend can act as wingman.
  - *Talent (才华)*: the finishing touch (画龙点睛), not the engine.
  - *Energy (能量)*: positive outlook, no complaining online or in person.
  - *Confidence (自信)*: independent of looks. Focus on one real strength rather than your weaknesses.
  - *Stability (稳定性)*: not swayed by her posts, reply delays or likes.
  - *Humour (幽默)*: only lands if some value backs it, otherwise you're "the monkey act".
  - *Leadership (领导力)*: decides and plans; she can follow.
- **Value felt > value held (价值感)**: what matters is the value she perceives. [plausible/untested. MANIPULATIVE when it means misrepresentation → honest version: present real value well]
- **Information-gap hobbies (信息差)**: hobbies that look expensive but aren't (driving range ~¥100–200). [plausible/untested as a talking point. Fine if you really do them; don't use them to imply wealth]
- **Show bright spots that attract who you want**: flaunting money attracts gold-diggers; lifestyle and character attract person-oriented women. Money should read as "not poor", not flaunted. [research-backed: conspicuous spending signals short-term intent]
- **Cars**: don't use a car to pursue. Supercars attract the wrong interest and scare off stable women. If you drive, a mid-range sedan signals maturity. A taxi keeps attention on you. "Women who liked you when you were poor really liked you." [research-backed directionally]
- **Job description (包装身份)**: describing your work in its highest-status honest form is fine. Calling a delivery rider a "self-media entrepreneur" isn't. [MANIPULATIVE → honest version: describe what you do truthfully, with enthusiasm]
- **App gate questions**: when an app makes you answer her screening question ("describe your ideal relationship"), don't write a generic romantic scene. Answer with a real view ("Honestly, the person matters more than the scene"). [plausible/untested]
- **First 3 lines diagnostic**: if she doesn't reply within the first 3 messages, the problem is your photos/profile, not your texting. [plausible/untested]

## 3. Approach / opening

### In person
- **Lower defences first (破除防备)**: her first fear is a salesperson or a man who won't leave. A time-box removes it: "Can I steal 30 seconds?" / "3 minutes". [research-backed: reducing perceived cost raises compliance]
- **Don't say "I'm not selling anything"**: it sounds like a salesperson. Say who you are quickly instead. [plausible/untested]
- **Give a reason**: requests with a reason, even a thin one, get more compliance. Honest: "I'm trying to get a photo here and you clearly know how to frame a shot. Would you mind?" or "I saw you and wanted to say hi." [research-backed]
- **Describe, don't gush (引起注意 → 描述状态 → 要微信)**: three-step opener. Get attention, describe the situation ("I was just walking over there and saw you…"), then ask for contact. Avoid "You're so elegant" as an opener. [plausible/untested]
- **Give before you ask (提供价值再索取)**: offer something first (take her photo too, offer to edit it, a recommendation), then make a small ask. [research-backed: reciprocity]
- **Favour → small repayment → bigger plan (先求助再回报)**: she does a small favour, you repay with something tiny (a ¥10 milk tea, not hotpot), and hanging out now has a natural reason. Big gestures to a stranger feel like a trap. [research-backed: foot-in-the-door]
- **Mutual repayment line**: "Isn't that how people get closer, by returning favours? (关系不就是互相报答才能拉近吗)". [plausible/untested. Keep it a joke, never an obligation]
- **Target selection (挑人)**: choose people with time and openness (already taking photos at a tourist spot, browsing). Skip anyone in work clothes rushing home. [plausible/untested]
- **Observe first**: watch for a while to infer her situation (solo tourist, free afternoon) and adapt. [plausible/untested. Don't follow or loiter]
- **Make her laugh fast**: if the first few lines don't make her smile she'll leave. Front-load the fun. [plausible/untested]
- **Anxiety drill (问路)**: ask a man for directions, then immediately approach a woman in the same relaxed state. Removes the "agenda" that causes freezing. [plausible/untested; exposure practice is research-backed]
- **Outcome independence**: "If it doesn't work, next." Expect failures; survivor bias makes other coaches look magic. [research-backed: exposure]
- **Honest admission of intent**: "I went through all that just to get your WeChat" can land well if you're fun. "Revealing your purpose is fine if you're fun; fatal if you're needy or boring." [plausible/untested]
- **Agenda-free appreciation**: "People want to be liked, not taken from (人希望被喜欢，不希望被索取)." You can show you like her as long as you aren't extracting something. [research-backed]
- **Calm, don't annoy, don't take, thick skin (沉稳 + 别烦别人 + 别索取 + 脸皮厚)**. [plausible/untested. Thick skin means resilience to rejection, not persisting past a no]
- **Pretext personas (travel blogger, vlogger, "street magician", "life recorder", buying a gift for a friend, interview "for a small gift", follower counts, famous-influencer connections)**: [MANIPULATIVE → honest version: use your real reason. "I liked your vibe and wanted to say hi" or a genuine photo request. Do not use]
- **Open schemes / bets (顶级阳谋)**: a bet where both outcomes give you a date ("If I don't have 5M followers I'll buy you meals for a month"). [MANIPULATIVE when built on false claims → honest version: playful real bets ("If I guess your star sign in three tries, you pick the dessert place")]
- **Hotspot / power-bank / phone-unlock "magic trick" / table-share pretexts**: [MANIPULATIVE → do not use. The phone-unlock trick adds yourself to her phone without real consent. Honest table share: ask plainly, keep your word on "5 minutes", don't relocate to her side]
- **Fake ankle sprain to get support / touch**: [MANIPULATIVE → do not use]
- **Begging to keep her ("求求你了")**: [MANIPULATIVE → if she declines, thank her and leave]
- **Decide for her when she hesitates (替她做决定 / "犹豫了就是同意")**: [MANIPULATIVE → honest version: lead on logistics once she has said yes. Hesitation is not consent; ask, and accept a no]
- **Guess games for contact**: "Three guesses at your star sign/job; if I'm right, we swap WeChat." [plausible/untested. Fine if she's clearly playing along; no prop "forces" or tricks]
- **Cold reading (冷读)**: stating her personality from cues ("cold outside, warm inside", "you'd keep an injured stray cat"). Only at ≥80% confidence, otherwise you look silly. [plausible/untested. Be honest that you're guessing]
- **Leave her wanting more**: end an approach with a plan or promise ("I'll message you tonight"). Her asking "how long do I wait?" is a strong positive. [plausible/untested]

### Apps / social
- **Minimal opener**: "hi", her name, or an emoji. No "美女", no "在干嘛", no lookalike pickup lines. An emoji-only first message also tests whether your profile passed. [plausible/untested]
- **Move platforms quickly**: apps can unmatch anytime. Move to WeChat with something short ("this app's clunky, add me on WeChat"). [plausible/untested]
- **Idol-lookalike compliment**: if her Moments show a celebrity she likes, saying she resembles them (for looks-type idols, not talent-type) lands. [plausible/untested. Drop the "rename contact + screenshot" gimmick if it feels like a script]
- **Topic from her profile, not your memory**: look at her photos, emoji, last line and comment within ~3 seconds. "Making up topics from nothing turns into a census." [plausible/untested]

## 4. Conversation craft

- **Three-stage order (吸引 → 拉升 → 邀约)**: attraction, then raising intimacy, then invitation. Chat is about half the game, the date the other half. [plausible/untested]
- **Greasy = timing (油腻 = 跳环节)**: a stage-N line said at stage N−1 is creepy. The same words are sweet from a spouse. When something fails, ask which stage you skipped. [plausible/untested; matches norms of incremental disclosure]
- **Attitude over content (态度 / 行为模式)**: state, don't plead. "Let's grab dinner next weekend" vs "could you give me a chance?". "I don't usually cook; we'll see how you behave" vs eager boasting. [plausible/untested]
- **Concise (言简意赅)**: strip filler and particles ("在干嘛" not "在干嘛呀"; "watching TV" not "I'm at home watching TV hehe"). Cool tone, few 呀/呢/吗. [plausible/untested]
- **Hook → display → release (钩子-展示-释放)**: hook ("I'm only just back"), display ("was in Italy with friends"), then change topic. 3–5 rounds build curiosity. Claimed bite rate ~60% (80% for "just got back"). [plausible/untested]
- **Switch cards (换牌)**: if a hook doesn't bite, try another "card" (tennis → cooking story). After ~4 misses, stop hooking if other interest signals are fine. [plausible/untested]
- **Never chain displays (连环展示)**: one display, then a joke or a question. Chains read as peacocking and approval-seeking. [plausible/untested]
- **Auto-attacks plus skills (平A加技能)**: plain small talk ~50–60% of the time, with a tease, hook or story every 3–5 lines. [plausible/untested]
- **Four topic types**: screen her (筛选), display yourself (展示), tease (调侃), small talk (闲聊). Self-deprecation was dropped from the set as status-lowering. [plausible/untested; light self-deprecation is fine in research, heavy isn't]
- **Information density (信息密度 / 抛钩子)**: never answer in two words. "Northeast" → "Northeast, which is why I'm weirdly good at sledding and useless with spicy food." "Any question she asks, tell a story." [research-backed: elaboration raises liking]
- **Talk about people, not events (不要聊事要聊人)**: "Can you cook?" beats "Did you have lunch?". Cooking opens imagined future scenes (you cook, she washes up). [plausible/untested]
- **Don't interview (查户口)**: a string of age/job/hometown questions is a census. Ask one question and react ("Aries? Would an Aries and a Gemini get along?"). [research-backed]
- **Screening (筛选 / 反筛选)**: ask what she brings ("Can you cook?") rather than only displaying yourself. It invites her to invest. Don't screen twice in a row without giving something of yourself. [plausible/untested]
- **Induce her to describe you (引导追逐)**: "So what do I look like to you?" She builds a picture of you, which raises her investment. [plausible/untested]
- **Dig deeper (刨根问底)**: follow up her volunteered topics with "why?". It's not interrogation if she raised it. [research-backed: escalating mutual self-disclosure builds closeness]
- **Curiosity replies (好奇撩法)**: "Really? Then what? No way!" keeps her sharing. [research-backed when the curiosity is sincere. MANIPULATIVE if feigned → honest version: ask only about what interests you]
- **Keyword association (关键词联想法)**: from a fact she mentions, picture the scene and go to the emotion ("kindergarten teacher" → patient, or secretly strict? soft-hearted?). Aim at feelings (loneliness, romance, joy), not logistics or salary. [plausible/untested]
- **Find topics in front of you (看话题，右脑)**: her ring, hair, lipstick, surroundings. Use stored knowledge only to colour it ("Sanya's sea vs Qingdao's"). [plausible/untested]
- **Remember her details**: reusing something she said earlier is a bonus. [research-backed: perceived responsiveness]
- **Read the emotion in a word**: "又开心了" (happy *again*) tells you she was down earlier. [plausible/untested]
- **Specific compliments (二级反馈 / 夸 not 捧)**: praise plus the reason: "You lost weight? Your face looks sharper, and purple suits you." People plant details hoping they'll be noticed. Generic flattery ("you look good in anything") is sucking up. [research-backed]
- **Indirect compliments (侧面夸 / 变相夸赞)**: praise through a reason or a third party ("Good-looking people always take good photos", "The stall owner gave you extra because you're pretty"). Use sparingly, not every line. [plausible/untested]
- **Subjectless compliment (不带主语的夸)**: "Pretty girls get approached a lot; really beautiful ones, nobody dares." It leaves a playful exit ("I didn't say it was you"). [plausible/untested]
- **Compliment plus slight self-raise (变相夸自己)**: don't only put her on a pedestal. "You'd have to try it to know, or you'll miss out." [plausible/untested]
- **Exaggerated, joking praise beats timid praise**: timid praise with an obvious aim reads as servile. [plausible/untested]
- **Have opinions (独立观念)**: hold your own views on values, gender roles and money, and disagree kindly. ~60% overlap is enough. "Harmony without sameness (君子和而不同)". Filtering out incompatible people is a feature (宁缺毋滥). [research-backed]
- **Don't defend weaknesses (不辩解缺点)**: if she teases your height or weight, redirect to a strength ("my attribute points went into wisdom"). [plausible/untested]
- **Tease (调侃)**: light teasing from a relaxed position. Keep it mild and stop if she isn't laughing. [plausible/untested]
- **Push-pull (推拉)**: one sentence that both praises and teases ("Do all pretty girls judge by looks?"). Pure tease = fight; pure praise = simp. [plausible/untested in small doses; as a strategy, see §7 note on intermittent reinforcement]
- **Mystery (神秘感)**: don't dump all your cards. Early attraction is partly her projection. "I have other work; the rest you'll find out." [plausible/untested; withholding basic facts for long contradicts disclosure research]
- **Imply, don't state, persona (暗示人设)**: show traits through stories, like a magician rolling up his sleeves. Never say "I'm not a player"; it makes you sound like one. [plausible/untested]
- **Value timing (吹牛找契机，点到为止)**: brag only when the conversation opens a door ("Can you handle spicy food?" → "I travel a lot"), in one or two lines, then stop. [plausible/untested]
- **Release availability (释放可得性 / 铁汉柔情)**: once attraction is there, show you're reachable ("Work, honestly. Not going to pretend I'm busier than that. No girlfriend either.") and show a softer domestic side (dog, cooking). Too much hard value reads as unreachable. [plausible/untested]
- **Preference with standards (偏爱 + 高标准 / 唯一性)**: "I don't do this for everyone" paired with "I'm picky". One alone reads as simp ("only you") or player ("central air-conditioner"). [research-backed: selective liking is preferred to indiscriminate liking]
- **"Firsts" (第一次)**: "I've never done this for anyone." [MANIPULATIVE when false → honest version: say it only if it's true]
- **Third-person escalation (第三人称拉升)**: attribute compatibility to a fortune-teller, tarot or a bystander ("a fortune-teller said…"). Lowers visible neediness. [plausible/untested]
- **Shared future imagery / planting expectations (暗示性邀约)**: "I'll cook for you sometime, if you behave." [plausible/untested]
- **Verbal touch (语言禁摩)**: describe touch in words, with her as the actor ("Would you hit me?" rather than "I'll hit you"; "come rub my shoulders" without "I'll give you…"). Short, no emoji, and only once she clearly likes you. [plausible/untested]
- **Reverse statement (反过来说)**: state a want negatively to lower neediness ("I rarely go shopping with women"). [plausible/untested]
- **Ex topics (前任话题)**: tell a short story or take a stance. Never badmouth an ex; don't interrogate hers. Never trash other women. [research-backed]
- **No third parties**: don't discuss other men or other women you're seeing during flirting. [plausible/untested]
- **Shared vulnerability (同病相怜)**: with a guarded woman, a real disclosure of your own hurt lowers defences. [research-backed when true. MANIPULATIVE when scripted from her disclosures → honest version: share only real experience]
- **Feigned naivety ("你骗我我也信你")**: [plausible/untested as a joke. Don't build a pose]
- **Information asymmetry about places (知识面打法)**: you can talk about places you've read about, phrased as "I'd love to go", not "I've been". [plausible/untested; honest only with that phrasing]

## 5. Tests / challenges

- **Triage every line: test / bait / window**: a test (测试) is a challenge to see if you get rattled: stay relaxed. Bait (引导跪舔/引导自证) invites you to prove yourself: toss it back lightly. A window (窗口) is real openness: move forward. [plausible/untested. Coach override: a real question gets a real answer]
- **Tease the teasing, answer the questions**: "How many people are you talking to?" asked sincerely deserves a straight answer. When unsure, answer honestly and lightly. That passes both. [research-backed: defensiveness hurts; honesty builds trust]
- **Don't write defensive essays (不解释/不自证)**: a long anxious defence in response to a light tease reads as insecure. [research-backed for defensiveness. Contradicted as "never explain anything"]
- **Reflect (反问)**: "Why haven't you asked me out?" → "Did you miss me?" [plausible/untested]
- **Choose C (选C / 跳出框架)**: asked A or B, give a third answer. "Where should I travel next?" → "Who you go with matters more than where." "Reward or punishment?" → "A memory." [plausible/untested]
- **Throw your own frame (抛框架)**: "Are you flirting?" → "I never flirt; I just go with how it feels." [plausible/untested]
- **Own it with a smile (大胆承认)**: "Are you flirting?" → "Shouldn't I be?" Don't deny something true. [research-backed: candour]

## 13. Relationship maintenance & conflict

- **Translate the accusation into the need (翻译需求)**: "You don't care about me" = "I want you to care more." "Why don't you just die out there" = "Come home earlier." Answer the need. [research-backed: listening for the underlying bid]
- **Read the heat**: intense anger usually means she's still invested; cold silence is the exit sign. [plausible/untested]
- **Name what's underneath**: "I think you know I like you, but you don't feel secure, so you keep checking." Ask for a concrete example. Being understood calms people more than being argued with. [research-backed]
- **Turn her logic gently**: "We talk every night; why doesn't that feel like I like you?" [plausible/untested; keep it curious, not adversarial]
- **Kindness with edges (包容+底线, the diamond)**: smooth on top, sharp underneath. "Tell me what you need directly; if it's reasonable I'm in. But threatening to break up every argument crosses my line." [research-backed: boundaries plus warmth]
- **Offer a face-saving step, then lead (给台阶 + 下指令)**: "Feeling better? I'll come pick you up now." [plausible/untested; with her agreement]
- **Don't panic at "let's break up"**: calmly respect her choice ("Whatever you decide, I'll respect it"). [research-backed: calm, respectful responses de-escalate]
- **Apologise when you're wrong**: "Never apologise / know your error, fix it, don't admit it (知错改错不认错)" is [contradicted by research → a sincere, specific apology rebuilds trust]
- **Reverse accusation ("you're too controlling" — 反咬一口)**: [MANIPULATIVE → do not use when she has a valid concern]
- **Lawyer moves (谁主张谁举证, 行为性质定义, 利益捆绑)**: [MANIPULATIVE when used to dodge accountability → honest version: explain transparently, show the messages if appropriate, agree on boundaries with coworkers]
- **Stonewalling to make her chase**: [contradicted by research; stonewalling predicts breakup. Take a time-out, then come back]
- **Intermittent warmth (忽冷忽热, 只行动不表达, 抽离)**: [contradicted by research for long-term: breeds anxiety. Be consistently warm]
- **Gifts don't fix problems**: buying a bag buys a response to the bag. Fix the attraction or trust issue, not the favour ledger ("fix A, not B"). [plausible/untested]
- **Career balance**: "Tell her your career matters more than her" is [contradicted by research as a rule → honest version: protect work time and say so plainly; show she matters too]
- **Unpredictability as maintenance ("the more uncomfortable she is, the more she likes you")**: [MANIPULATIVE → do not use]
- **Usage rights / keep options mid-relationship**: [MANIPULATIVE once exclusive → do not use]
- **Long-term base**: authenticity, consistency, potential, taking responsibility. Partners stay through hard times for character, not cash. [research-backed]

## 14. Money

- **80/20/0**: 80% on yourself (clothes, fitness, skills, experiences), 20% on shared experiences, 0% on gifts, bags, transfers or "investments" in her to win her. [plausible/untested; shared experiences are research-backed]
- **Why 0% gifts**: gifts buy a reaction to the gift. If it fails you feel cheated and may become bitter (stalker-ish behaviour). Credit goes to whoever was present for the feeling, not whoever paid. [research-backed directionally: experiences > material goods for closeness]
- **"Money is to be shown, not spent on her"**: [plausible/untested. The honest version: let your lifestyle speak, don't use money as bait]
- **Spend less to succeed more**: heavy spending before you're together signals she needn't commit and keeps you stuck in ambiguity. [plausible/untested]
- **Quality over quantity**: ¥300 for three good dishes beats eight mediocre ones; one great experience beats five dinners. [plausible/untested]
- **Don't play poor**: "I saved three months for this" signals she'll share the burden later. Give as if it's no big deal, or don't give. [plausible/untested]
- **Don't flash early**: supercar or luxury displays signal short-term interest. [research-backed]
- **Cheap dates work**: a whole street date can cost ~¥40. A ~¥280 dinner date in the cases. [plausible/untested]
- **Need-cup model (需求刻度杯)**: each person "fills" a 100-point need set (looks, status/vanity, sexual value, EQ/humour, money); gaps get filled with demands, gifts and service — a bottomless pit. Raising your own value lowers the extras she asks for. [plausible/untested; drop point weights]
- **Shared consumption vs investment**: paying for things you do together is fine; "investing" in her (clothes, transfers) raises her market value, and she may trade up. [plausible/untested. Rusbult's investment model predicts the opposite for committed couples]
- **Rare kindness (稀有才珍贵)**: generic kindness (good-morning texts, "drink hot water", ¥20 delivery) doesn't stand out. Kindness lands after attraction, and with thresholds. [plausible/untested]
- **Unconditional big kindness reads as a motive**: "a weasel bearing New Year gifts". Start small, ask a small favour first, then treat. [research-backed: unsolicited large favours create suspicion and pressure]
- **Look rich until it's too late ("生米煮成熟饭")**: [MANIPULATIVE → do not use]
