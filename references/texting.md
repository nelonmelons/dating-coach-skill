## 1. Ground rules

- **Short.** Two or three lines, one idea per message. Cut the hedges: "haha", "lol", "just wondering", "sorry if this is weird", trailing "~". In Chinese, drop the softening particles (在干嘛 not 在干嘛呀).
- **Texture, not two words.** "Where are you from?" → "the north, which is why I'm great at sledding and useless with spicy food." Give something they can grab.
- **People, not events.** Questions about who they are ("are you a morning person or a liar?") beat logistics ("how was your day").
- **One question, then react.** Don't stack three questions. React to the answer with an opinion, tease or story before asking anything else.
- **Callbacks.** Referencing something they said earlier is the cheapest proof you were listening.
- **Small talk is fine.** About half of any conversation is plain chat. Drop a "skill" (tease, hook, story) every few lines, not every line.
- **End on a high.** Leave while it's fun: "ok I have to go be productive, continue this later". Let them have the last word sometimes.
- **Texting is for logistics and anticipation, not for building the whole relationship.** Attraction is built in person. Don't spend your best material in text.

## 2. Reply timing

- The sweet spot is *normal*: not instant to everything, not strategic silence. Some delay signals a life; responsiveness (feeling attended to) is one of the strongest drivers of closeness. The simple model has an interior optimum, so the honest rule is "reply when it's natural, roughly at their pace".
- **Mirror the pace.** If they take an hour, you don't need to reply in 30 seconds. If they reply fast and warmly, it's fine to be quicker.
- Don't reply when they've told you they're busy (driving, working, gaming); answer when they're free so the message gets attention.
- Deliberate long delays (hours or days) are only defensible as a response to low-effort or testing messages early on. After a good date or once interest is mutual, games read as cooling off.
- Never comment on their reply time ("took you long enough"). Never apologise for yours unless it was actually rude.

## 3. Reading interest over text

**Window / green signals:** remembering a small detail you mentioned days or weeks ago; longer replies than yours; laughter and filler ("hahaha", "wait really??"); emojis; unprompted updates about their day ("ugh so tired"); asking you questions; asking your name or plans; sharing photos; keeping a dying thread alive; playful annoyance at your silence ("did you vanish?"); suggesting plans themselves.

**Yellow signals:** deflection of a flirt ("are you flirting with me haha"); short but polite replies; answering without asking back; "maybe" to an invite without a counter-offer. Yellow means slow down, go back to rapport, try again later — not push harder.

**Red signals:** one-word replies over several days; repeated "busy" with no alternative offered; no reply to a soft invite and the next message; blocked/deleted. Red means stop investing. Most refusals are sincere.

Weigh the trend over a few exchanges. Reply length ratio and who initiates are the two most informative numbers.

## 4. Moving from chat to a date

- **Diagnose first.** No reply in the first 3 messages → it's the profile/photos/opener, not the texting. Replies then fade → the conversation. Chatting for weeks without meeting → you never invited. Rule of thumb: invite after a good run of a few days (3–7) or ~20 good exchanges; more than three rounds of chat without an invite is a stalled flow.
- **Check availability before inviting:** "what are you up to this weekend?" — lets you avoid walking into a no.
- **Soft invite first:** "we should get [thing you talked about] sometime". Costs nothing if it misses.
- **If warm, get specific immediately:** "Thursday 7? there's a ramen place near you I've been meaning to try" + location pin. A concrete plan is far easier to accept than "when are you free?".
- **"Incidental" framing lowers pressure:** "I'm getting Thai food anyway, come along".
- **Lead, but don't command a stranger.** Confident and specific ("let's do Thursday") beats asking permission ("would you maybe possibly want to…"); still leave room to say no.
- **If they say no but offer another time,** it's a yes — lock it in. If they say no with no alternative, one more try later at most; two misses and you leave the ball with them: "no worries — let me know when you're free".

## 5. After a date and between dates

**Same night or next morning:** one short, warm text. Callback to a moment from the date beats generic thanks.
> had a really good time tonight. still not over your [specific opinion/story] 😂

If the date went badly or was flat, a short, kind close is fine, or nothing.

**Between date 1 and date 2 (date already set):**
- Text, but lightly. One or two light touches across the week — a callback, something that reminded you of them, a quick bit of banter. Not a daily all-day chat.
- Be the one who wraps conversations up while they're good.
- Match their energy: if they're chatty, you can be a bit more; if quieter, stay light. Never double-text an unanswered message.
- Confirm the plan a day or two before with specifics (time, place, pin). Leading logistics is part of the attraction.
- Don't over-read a slow reply ("stability"). Don't show up with nothing left to talk about.
- Skip artificial delays now. Uncertainty only helps at the very start; once both are clearly interested, it reads as cooling off.

**After date 1 with no next date set:** within a day, send the warm text and propose the next plan while the feeling is fresh — "I owe you a rematch at [game]. Saturday?" Don't wait a week.

**Between later dates (2–3+):** contact can rise naturally with warmth — that's the healthy arc (measured at first, warmer as interest becomes mutual). Daily contact is fine if both are doing it. Keep the plans coming; momentum matters more than message volume.

## 6. Silence, fading and re-opening

- **Chase once.** If a message goes unanswered, one light follow-up after a day or two. Then stop.
- **Make the follow-up easy to answer:** a new topic, a photo, a playful line — not "?", "hello??", or "did I do something wrong".
- **A dead chat isn't permanent.** A single awkward exchange is usually forgotten within a week or so (rule of thumb, untested). Re-open after about a week with something light and new, not a reference to the silence:
> saw this and immediately thought of your [thing] rant
- **Before re-opening someone who went cold,** spend a week making your social presence reflect your real life (what you actually do, friends, hobbies; delete sad or bragging posts). That's the "display surface" they'll check.
- **If they deleted or blocked you, that's the answer.** Never re-add under another account.
- **Accept a soft no.** "I've been so busy" twice with no alternative → let it go with grace: "all good, have a great week". It leaves the door open and keeps your self-respect.

## 7. Script bank

Adjust to the user's voice. Never send these verbatim if they sound unlike the user.

- **Post-date:** "home safe? had a great time" / "10/10 company, 6/10 dumplings, we need a rematch"
- **Midweek touch:** "[callback] update: I tried it. you were right. I hate that you were right"
- **Confirm:** "still on for saturday? 7 at [place], I'll grab a table" + pin
- **Soft invite:** "you'd love this place, we should go" / "ok we have to settle this over coffee"
- **Specific invite:** "Thursday after work? there's a wine bar near [area]"
- **Check availability:** "what's your weekend looking like?"
- **Follow-up after silence:** a photo + one line; a new observation; a light callback. No guilt.
- **Exiting:** "ok I'm being summoned, to be continued"
- **Graceful close:** "no worries at all, good luck with everything"

## 8. Diagnosing a chat log

When the user shares a conversation, work it through and answer in this shape (compress for short chats):

1. **Where it stands** — stage, and the trend (warming / flat / cooling) in one line.
2. **Their signals** — quote their actual words for the 2–4 most telling lines and say green / yellow / red. Read against *their own baseline*: a naturally brief texter writing three lines is a bigger signal than a chatty one doing it. Who initiates and the message-length ratio are the two most informative facts.
3. **What you're doing wrong (if anything)** — in order of cost: neediness signs (essays, stacked questions, double texts, instant replies at 2am), interview mode, missed invite moments, defensive answers to teases. Skip this section if they're doing fine; don't invent faults.
4. **Send this** — one main reply, plus one or two variants (e.g. playful / straightforward), each with a few words on its intent and the likely response.
5. **Next 1–3 moves** — with timing and branches (if warm → …, if short → …, if silent → chase once, then exit ladder in `recovery.md`).
