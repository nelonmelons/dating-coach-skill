# Recovery, exits and graceful failure

Use when interest is fading, someone has said no, a date went badly, or the user is stuck deciding whether to keep trying.

## 1. The exit ladder

Use observable triggers, not gut feel or made-up "interest scores".

| Tier | Triggers | What the user does |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Cool down** | Replies shortening over several days; a soft invite missed; one flake with no reschedule | Stop initiating for about a week. Live your life; post real things. Then one light, new re-opener (texting.md §6). |
| **2. Graceful close** | Re-opener ignored or answered flatly; two declined invites with no alternative; "I'm so busy" twice | Send one warm close and leave the door open: "all good — been fun talking, good luck with everything." Then stop. |
| **3. Full stop** | Explicit no; "not interested"; blocked or deleted; asked not to be contacted | No further contact. Forbidden: second accounts, messages through friends, showing up in person, liking old posts to get attention, "just one last message". |

If they come back on their own at any tier, respond warmly and normally — no punishment, no interrogation — and move toward a plan.

## 2. Recovering a cooled interaction

1. **Find which stage failed** (SKILL.md stage model). Never replied → profile/first impression. Faded mid-chat → conversation. Cooled after a date → the date or escalation pace.
2. **Two jobs:** show real improvement and let the bad moment fade. About a week of silence while your social presence reflects your actual life.
3. **Re-open simply:** a plain "hi", a callback, or something new and light. Not "what are you doing", not "hey beautiful", not "sorry if I was weird".
4. **One message at a time.** If it lands, rebuild rapport before any invite. If not, go to tier 2.

## 3. Graceful-failure scripts

- **Rejected but you share a workplace, class or friend group:** acknowledge once, kindly, then act completely normal. "Totally get it — no weirdness on my end." Never relitigate; don't avoid them; don't recruit friends.
- **Confessed feelings too early and got a lukewarm answer:** "fair — I just like saying what I think. no pressure at all." Then go back to being fun and low-key; let actions rebuild, don't repeat the confession.
- **"You're really nice but…":** believe it. "Appreciate you being straight with me." Don't argue the case.
- **"I'm not looking for anything right now":** believe it at face value. If the user wants something serious, exit gracefully; if they're genuinely happy as friends, fine — but not as a waiting strategy.
- **The date was flat:** a short kind text ("thanks for tonight, nice meeting you") or nothing if they've signalled no interest. Don't chase a flat date with a big plan.
- **They bring a friend unexpectedly:** be warm and include the friend; it's a vetting moment. Don't sulk and don't flirt with the friend to provoke jealousy.
- **Running into an ex (or their ex) at a party:** brief, polite, then return to your own group. No performance, no drama.
- **You did something awkward on the date:** own it lightly once ("that joke died a horrible death"), don't spiral.

## 4. When they vent or are upset

Don't fix, joke or one-up first. Reflect and validate, then ask what would help:
> ugh that sounds exhausting. do you want advice or do you want me to be outraged with you
Then follow their lead. Responsiveness here builds more closeness than any flirting.

## 5. Talking the user through sunk cost

- "But we talked for weeks" → time spent isn't evidence of interest; the last few exchanges are.
- "One more try" → if tiers 2–3 triggered, another try costs self-respect and makes a later return less likely.
- "Maybe she's testing me" → refusals are not tests; treat a no as a no.
- **The week after:** see friends, do something you're good at, talk to someone new. Abundance cures neediness; rejection is selection, not a verdict.
