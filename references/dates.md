## 1. Planning the date

- **You lead logistics.** Pick the time and place. Ask only preferences ("sweet or spicy?", "bar or coffee?"). "Wherever you want" hands them the work.
- **Movement over sitting.** Three to five short stops in about three hours, ~40 minutes each: walk → drink → game/arcade/market → dinner → bar. Shared novel, mildly exciting activities raise closeness, and the good feeling attaches to whoever was there.
- **Skip the cinema on date one.** Ninety minutes of silence in the dark.
- **First-date venue checklist:** easy to talk, a bit dim and cosy, seats where you can sit side by side or at a corner, not too formal or expensive, walkable to a second spot. Small Japanese/izakaya-style places, wine bars, dessert spots, night markets work well.
- **Plan the first laugh.** Have an easy, funny opener or observation ready so they laugh in the first 30 seconds. First impressions are like the first dish.
- **Have a plan B nearby** in case the venue is bad or packed.
- **Keep it short-ish.** End while it's still good rather than letting it run until you're both tired.

## 2. On the date

- **Sit beside, not across**, or at a corner. If seated across, "come sit here, I can't hear you" is fine.
- **Don't wait on them.** Serving food, hovering, over-thanking reads as waiter energy. Be generous and relaxed instead.
- **Conversation:** people not events, hook → display → release, follow-ups with "why?", opinions, specific compliments, matched self-disclosure that deepens over time.
- **Leave bad venues or films.** Don't let sunk cost set the mood: "this place is dead, let's go find dessert".
- **Late? Own it once:** "sorry, that's on me — next round's on me too." No excuses, no essay.
- **Phones away.** Full attention is the cheapest attractive thing you can do.
- **Read the window in person:** leaning in, holding eye contact, touching your arm, laughing a lot, asking about you, prolonging the date, suggesting the next stop.

## 3. Escalation and consent

- **Three small before one big.** Small: a flirty line, a playful nickname, a brief light touch (2–3 seconds on the arm). Big: holding hands, a kiss, inviting them over. If the small ones land, continue. If they deflect, that's a yellow light — slow down and go back to rapport.
- **Let them complete the move.** "Let me see your hand" (to read a palm or compare sizes) — they choose to place it there.
- **Lean in → continue; pull back even slightly → stop and stay friendly.** No sulking.
- **When unsure, ask.** "Can I kiss you?" doesn't kill the mood; it usually raises comfort.
- **Inviting them back to your place:** only after clear in-person green signals, with a real, low-key reason and an easy out: "want to come back to mine? I'll make the [thing] I was bragging about — totally fine if not." If they hesitate: "all good, let me walk you to the train," and end on a high. How you take a no shapes the next yes. Never after pushing drinks, and not if they've had too much.
- **Declare interest plainly when the time comes.** Not begging for a verdict ("will you please be my girlfriend?") and not pretending there's no decision — say what you want with confidence and leave room for their answer.
- **Never:** alcohol as a lever, pressure, ultimatums ("now or I'm leaving"), treating hesitation as yes, treating a no as a test. Most refusals are sincere; consent requires treating "no" as decisive.

## 4. Ending the date

- End on a high, ideally with a light reference to next time ("you still owe me that dumpling place").
- Make sure they get home OK; a short "home safe?" text is good.
- If it went well, the next plan can be floated in person or within a day by text.

## 5. Dates 2 and 3

- **Momentum matters.** Aim for the next date within about a week. Long gaps let interest decay.
- **Rising warmth arc.** Measured at first, warmer as interest becomes mutual. The research supports a single cool-to-warm arc, not endless hot-and-cold.
- **Escalate the setting gradually:** date 2 can be more active or personal (cooking class, hike, their neighbourhood, a place you love); date 3 more intimate (cooking together, a quieter bar, your place for dinner if it fits).
- **Deepen disclosure.** Move from fun facts to values, ambitions, what they care about. Ask, then share back at the same depth.
- **Introduce your life.** Your friends, hobbies, projects. "Project first, person second": invite them into a life that already exists.
- **Rule of thumb:** most relationships form around date three if things are mutual; you don't need to rush, but don't stall in vague limbo either.

## 6. Becoming exclusive

- Signs it's time: regular contact both ways, plans every week, meeting friends, both initiating.
- Say it directly and calmly: "I'm not seeing anyone else and I don't want to — I'd like us to be exclusive. How do you feel?" Confident, not pleading, open to their answer.
- If they're not ready, believe them; decide what you want without ultimatums.

## 7. Flakes, lateness, cancellations

- **One cancellation with a reschedule offered** = normal life. Lock the new time.
- **Cancellation with no alternative:** short, easy, don't ask why: "no worries — let me know when works". Ball in their court. If nothing comes back, follow the exit ladder in `recovery.md` (about a week, then at most one light re-opener).
- **Warm but vague** ("so sorry!! next week for sure"): reply warmly and offer one concrete option: "all good — thursday or sunday?" If that also gets vague, stop proposing and let them come to you.
- **Two flakes:** treat it as low interest. Step back, keep living; if they come back with a concrete plan, great.
- **They're late:** be gracious once; repeated lateness without apology is information.
- **You need to cancel:** do it early, apologise once, propose a specific replacement.

## 8. Money (80/20/0)

- 80% of discretionary spend on yourself (clothes that fit, fitness, skills, experiences that make you more interesting), 20% on shared experiences (the dates), 0% on gifts or transfers to win someone over before you're together.
- Gifts from someone they aren't into yet create obligation, not attraction; shared experiences create closeness.
- Pay for the date if you invited (or split if that's the local norm and they insist — don't make it a fight).
- Don't "play poor" ("I saved for months for this") and don't flash (luxury cars and logos read as short-term signals).
- Small generosity framed as no big deal beats big gestures.
