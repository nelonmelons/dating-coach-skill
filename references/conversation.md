## 1. Presentation and profiles

- **Look put-together, not flashy.** Fit and fabric over logos; clean grooming (nose hair, nails, haircut); one good fragrance. Fewer fidgets and movements read as presence.
- **Photos:** 6–10 honest photos of you doing real things — a clear face shot, a full-body shot, a hobby, friends (where you're obviously identifiable), travel or an activity. No group shot first, no sunglasses-only, no mirror selfies as the lead.
- **Your feed is your résumé.** Before a date or re-contact, they'll look. Show your real life; delete sad, complaining or bragging posts.
- **Bio:** specific hooks they can reply to ("will judge your dumpling order") beat lists of adjectives.
- **Never:** stock photos, borrowed cars, "montage" posting to fake a lifestyle. The mismatch on meeting costs more than the photos earned.

## 2. Approaching in person

- **Pick people with time.** Someone browsing, sightseeing or waiting is open; someone rushing in work clothes isn't. A no from someone busy isn't personal.
- **Time-box:** "can I steal 30 seconds?" removes the fear you won't leave. Don't say "I'm not selling anything" — that's what salespeople say.
- **Give a true reason:** "I was walking past and noticed you…" / "you clearly know this area — where's good for coffee?" / "you look like you'd have a strong opinion on this". Describe, don't gush ("you're so beautiful" puts pressure on them).
- **Give before you ask** — a recommendation, a photo for them, a laugh — then a small ask, then a plan. Keep any thank-you small.
- **Aim for a laugh early.**
- **Close with a plan or a number,** not a vague "nice meeting you". If it's not there, "nice talking to you" and move on.
- **Warm-up for nerves:** ask a couple of people for directions first, then approach in the same relaxed state, with no agenda beyond a conversation.
- **The mindset:** if it doesn't work, it doesn't work. Next.

## 3. Apps and social DMs

- **Opener = something specific from their profile + a hook or question,** not "hey". "ok I need the story behind photo 3" / "controversial: [their stated opinion] is wrong and I can prove it".
- Respond to what they actually wrote with an opinion, not just a question.
- Move to a date within a few days of good chat (see texting.md §4). Apps are for setting up dates, not pen pals.
- On social media DMs: react to a story with a specific, light comment; don't like twenty old posts.

## 4. Conversation craft

- **Hook → display → release.** Hook: "I only just got back." Display (if they bite): "from Italy with friends." Release: change subject; don't elaborate unless asked. If a hook doesn't land, switch topic ("change cards"). One per few minutes; never stack.
- **Information density.** Answers with texture, never two words.
- **People, not events.** "Can you cook?" → an imagined future (you cook, they wash up — who's better?).
- **Don't interview.** Age/job/hometown in a row is a census. One question + reaction: "Aries? Hm. Would an Aries survive a road trip with a Gemini?"
- **Keyword association.** Grab a word and run with it: "kindergarten teacher… so you're either infinitely patient or secretly terrifying."
- **Find topics in front of you:** what they're wearing, what they just said, what's in their photos.
- **Follow with "why?"** Escalating, reciprocal self-disclosure is the most reliable way to create closeness.
- **Specific compliments ("second-order feedback"):** praise plus the reason. "That purple really works on you" beats "you look nice". Notice the details people plant hoping someone sees.
- **Have opinions.** Disagree sometimes, kindly. ~60% overlap in values is plenty; agreeing with everything reads as no self.
- **Humour** is one of the strongest attractors. Playful exaggeration, role-play, callbacks.
- **Mix small talk with "skills":** about half plain chat, with a tease, hook or story every few lines.
- **Assume familiarity.** Talk like you already get along; it's contagious.

## 5. Teases and "tests"

Triage what they said:

| Type | Looks like | Do |
|---|---|---|
| **Tease / test** | "you must say that to everyone", "you look like a player", "prove it" | Stay relaxed. Tease back, reframe, laugh. |
| **Bait to grovel** | "men are all talk", "convince me" | Don't start proving. Toss it back lightly. |
| **Real question** | "are you seeing other people?", "what are you looking for?" | Answer honestly and lightly. |
| **Window** | they share, ask about you, linger | Move forward. |

Moves for light challenges:
- **Reflect:** "Why haven't you asked me out?" → "did you miss me?"
- **Choose C:** offered A or B, give a third answer. "Where should I travel next?" → "who you go with matters more than where."
- **Redefine the label:** "You're a player." → "being good at talking isn't the same as being a player. A player lies; I just say what I think."
- **Flip the judge:** "You look like you hit on girls all the time." → "judging by looks? minus one point."
- **Own it with a smile:** "Are you flirting?" → "shouldn't I be?"
- **Agree and exaggerate:** "You're so weird." → "certified. I have papers."

Rule: **tease the teasing, answer the questions.** If unsure which it is, answer honestly and lightly — that passes both. Memorised comebacks stop working once they're common; real security is what survives repeated challenges, so build that rather than a script.

## 6. Reading interest in person

Green: sustained eye contact, laughing, turning toward you, touching your arm, asking about you, playing with hair/jewellery while talking to you, staying longer than planned, suggesting the next stop. Yellow: polite but closed body language, deflecting flirts, glancing at the phone or exit. Red: short answers, turning away, mentioning needing to leave, pulling back from touch. Respond to yellow by slowing down, red by being friendly and wrapping up.
