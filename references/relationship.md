# Relationships: repair and maintenance

## Conflict repair

- **Translate the accusation into the need.** "You don't care about me" usually means "I want you to care more". "Why don't you just stay out all night" means "come home earlier". Answer the need, not the attack: "you're right that I've been distracted this week — Friday is ours, phone off."
- **Name what's underneath.** "I think you know I like you, but you don't feel secure, so you keep checking." Being understood calms people more than being argued with.
- **Read the heat.** Intense anger usually means they're still invested. Cold indifference is the real exit sign.
- **Kindness with edges (the diamond).** Smooth on top, sharp underneath: "I'll meet you on anything reasonable, but threatening to break up in every argument is a line for me."
- **Apologise when you're wrong.** Specific, once, with a change: "I'm sorry I bailed last minute. That wasn't fair. I'll tell you earlier next time." Apology rebuilds trust; "never apologise" is folk advice that the research contradicts.
- **Avoid the four predictors of breakdown:** criticism of character, contempt, defensiveness, stonewalling. Don't go silent to punish; if you need a break, say when you'll come back to it.
- **Don't flip it into counter-accusation** ("you're the controlling one") to win the round.

## Busy schedules and "you never make time for me"

- Don't argue the hours; answer the need (to feel prioritised). Own the true part once, specifically.
- Protected and predictable beats more: one fixed evening a week phone-off, a short daily check-in at a set time, plans on the calendar in advance. Keep only promises you can keep.
- Be plain about work: "this quarter is brutal until March; here's what I can protect." Career and partner can both matter; hiding the trade-off breeds resentment.
- If breakup threats recur in every fight, set the boundary calmly (the diamond, below) once things are cooler.
- If after real changes the gap is about values (how much time a relationship should get, life direction), say so honestly — it may be a mismatch, not a scheduling problem.

## Keeping it alive

- **Novelty.** Couples who do new, mildly exciting things together report higher relationship quality. Rotate in new activities, trips, challenges.
- **Responsiveness.** Notice and react to bids for attention (a shared meme, a comment about their day). This is the daily currency of intimacy.
- **Project first still applies.** Keep your own friends, hobbies and goals. A partner who has a life is more attractive than one who orbits.
- **Self-respect.** If you're exhausted, say "I'm wiped tonight, Thursday?" Always-on availability reads as low self-respect and breeds resentment.
- **Don't try to "move" them with grand sacrifice.** Being moved isn't the same as liking; guilt isn't attraction.
- **Honesty about the long run.** Anything faked early (lifestyle, status) collapses over time; real self-improvement compounds.

## Insecurity and jealousy

- Distinguish their insecurity (reassure with actions and consistency, set a boundary on surveillance) from a real breach (talk about it directly).
- Don't over-read their social media, likes or reply times. Stability — not being knocked off balance by small signals — is attractive and protects you.

## Long-term milestones

- **Meeting friends and family:** friends first, low-key; family when you're both confident it's serious. Prepare them for each other.
- **Moving in, money, future plans:** talk explicitly about expectations (splitting costs, savings, where to live, kids, careers) before big steps. Mismatches found early are cheap.
- **Cultural expectations:** in Chinese-speaking families, housing, bride price (彩礼) and parents' involvement often come up before marriage; elsewhere other norms apply. Raise these as joint planning, not negotiation or tests of love.
- **Proposal readiness:** stable conflict repair, shared plans, and both people wanting it — not timeline pressure from outside.

## Breakups and rejection

- Rejection is selection, not a verdict on your worth.
- After a breakup, coach the person first: sleep, friends, routine, exercise, a project. No-contact for a while helps most people; it's for healing, not a tactic.
- Win-back attempts only make sense if the cause is fixable and both want it. Never pressure, never use jealousy, never re-contact after a block.
