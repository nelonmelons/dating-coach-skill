## 1
Text her during the week. Waiting for her to reach out to "play it cool" usually reads as low interest, especially after a date you both enjoyed.

You don't need to text all day. A few light touches is plenty:
- **Within a day of the first date (if you haven't already):** something short and specific, like "Had a really good time last night, the [thing you laughed about] still has me cracking up."
- **Midweek:** one or two casual messages. Send a meme or link tied to something you talked about, or ask how the thing she mentioned (work presentation, trip, game) went.
- **A day or two before Saturday:** confirm the plan. "Still on for Saturday? I was thinking [place] around 7, sound good?"

Match her energy. If she replies quickly and at length, it's fine to chat a bit more. If her replies are short, keep yours short too. Save the real conversation for the date. The texts are just there to keep things warm and show you're looking forward to seeing her.

## 4
Some ground rules first:
- **Check company policy.** Some workplaces restrict dating, especially if there's any reporting relationship. If one of you manages the other, don't do it.
- **Ask once.** If she says no, or gives a vague "maybe sometime," take that as a no and don't ask again.
- **Make it low pressure and private.** Not in a meeting, not in front of others, and ideally not over work Slack/email.

How to ask:
- Do it in person, or by personal text if you already have that kind of rapport.
- Be clear that it's a date, not a "team hang" she has to decode: "I've really enjoyed getting to know you. Would you want to get a drink with me sometime, outside of work?"
- Give her an easy out right away: "Totally fine if not, it won't make anything weird on my end."

If she says no:
- Say "No problem, thanks for being straight with me," and drop it.
- Then act completely normal. Be just as friendly and professional, don't avoid her, and don't bring it up again or tell coworkers. Things only get weird when the person who asked behaves differently afterward. If you act the same, it usually blows over within a day or two.

Be honest with yourself: if you'd struggle to stay normal after a rejection, think hard before asking.

Probably nothing dramatic. But three weeks of daily texting with every invite turned down is a pattern, and at this point her actions are answering the question.

Common reasons:
- She likes the attention and chatting but isn't interested in meeting.
- She's interested but not enough to make time.
- She's genuinely swamped. If so, though, she'd usually offer an alternative ("this week's crazy, but how about next Sunday?").

What to do:
1. **Make one clear, specific invite** with a real time and place: "I'd really like to actually meet you. Are you free Thursday or Saturday for a drink at [place]?"
2. **Watch whether she counter-offers.** Someone who's interested and busy will suggest another time. A plain "I'm busy" with no alternative is a soft no.
3. **If she dodges again,** say something like: "No worries. Sounds like you've got a lot going on. If you want to grab that drink sometime, let me know." Then stop texting every day and let her come to you.

What you might be doing wrong is the daily texting itself. Long pen-pal phases make meeting up feel less necessary. In the future, try to suggest meeting within the first week or so of chatting, while there's momentum.

This is serious. Repeated breakup threats usually mean she's felt unheard about this for a while, so it's worth treating as a real problem and not just a fight to get through.

**Start by listening, not defending.** Your instinct will be to explain the 60-hour weeks. She probably already knows that. What she wants to know is whether she's a priority. Sit down with her (not over text) and say something like: "I know you've felt like you come last, and I don't want you to feel that way. Can you tell me what's been hardest?" Then actually listen and reflect back what you hear before you explain anything.

**Then make concrete changes, not promises.** "I'll try harder" doesn't mean much after repeated conflict. Specific commitments do:
- A protected weekly date night that you don't cancel for work.
- A daily check-in, like a phone-free dinner or 20 minutes before bed.
- Small things during the workday: a call on your lunch break, a text that shows you're thinking of her.
- Ask her what would make the biggest difference. It might be different from what you'd guess.

**Look honestly at the bigger picture.** If the 60-hour weeks are temporary (a launch, a busy season), tell her when they end and plan something for after. If this is your permanent life, you both need to talk openly about whether it's sustainable for the relationship.

**Address the threats separately, once things are calmer.** It's fair to say: "I want to fix this, but it's hard when breaking up comes up every time we fight. Can we agree to talk through things without that?" Don't lead with this, or it'll sound like you're deflecting.

If you both want this to work but keep going in circles, a few sessions of couples counseling can really help.

I'd steer clear of the drinks idea. Getting her to drink more so she'll come home with you, after she's already said she wasn't sure, is using alcohol to get around her hesitation. That's not okay. It can also cross into her not being able to meaningfully consent, which is a serious ethical and legal line. "I think she was just being shy" is a guess. Treat "I'm not sure" as a no for now.

What actually works, and keeps things good between you:
- **Let her set the pace.** If she enjoys being with you, she'll be more open to it when she's ready, not when she's had more to drink.
- **Make an easy, no-pressure invite** near the end of a good date, while you're both reasonably sober: "Want to come back to mine for a bit? Totally fine if not."
- **Take a no gracefully.** "No worries, I had a great time tonight" makes you someone she feels safe with, which makes a future yes more likely.
- **Ask her directly** sometime when it's not in the moment: "Last time you seemed unsure about coming over. I want you to be comfortable, so no rush at all." Being open like this builds trust.

If she wants to come over, she'll say yes clearly. If she doesn't, no amount of drinks makes it okay.
