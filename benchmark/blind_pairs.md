# Case 1

**User message:**

first date went well, we set the second one for next saturday. should i text her during the week or wait for her to reach out?

## Response X

Text her, but lightly. Going silent until Saturday reads as cooling off, and flooding her all week burns the stuff you should be saving for the date.

**The plan:**
- **Tonight or tomorrow:** one short, warm text with a callback to something from the date.
- **Midweek:** one or two light touches. Something that reminded you of her, a quick bit of banter. You wrap it up while it's still fun.
- **Thursday or Friday:** confirm with a time, a place and a pin.

**Send:**
> had a really good time, still thinking about your [callback] take 😂

Midweek, something like:
> [callback] update: I tried it. you were right. I hate that you were right

Confirm:
> still on for saturday? 7 at [place], I'll grab a table

**Branches:**
- She's chatty → match her a bit more. Still be the one who ends it on a high.
- She's quieter → keep it light and don't read anything into it. She said yes to date two, and that tells you more than any one text.
- One message goes unanswered → don't double-text. Just confirm the plan later in the week.

Skip the "make her wait" reply delays. They only help at the very start. Once you've had a good date and she's agreed to a second, playing hard to get looks like you're losing interest.

## Response Y

Text her during the week. Waiting for her to reach out to "play it cool" usually reads as low interest, especially after a date you both enjoyed.

You don't need to text all day. A few light touches is plenty:
- **Within a day of the first date (if you haven't already):** something short and specific, like "Had a really good time last night, the [thing you laughed about] still has me cracking up."
- **Midweek:** one or two casual messages. Send a meme or link tied to something you talked about, or ask how the thing she mentioned (work presentation, trip, game) went.
- **A day or two before Saturday:** confirm the plan. "Still on for Saturday? I was thinking [place] around 7, sound good?"

Match her energy. If she replies quickly and at length, it's fine to chat a bit more. If her replies are short, keep yours short too. Save the real conversation for the date. The texts are just there to keep things warm and show you're looking forward to seeing her.

# Case 2

**User message:**

matched w this girl on hinge 5 days ago, convo's been good but she's replying slower now. last bit:
   me: lol ok so whats ur ideal sunday
   her: sleep till 11 and brunch obvi
   me: thats so valid, where do u usually go for brunch? do u cook too? whats ur fav food
   her: depends haha
   me: haha fair
   (no reply for a day). what do i send

## Response X

It's cooling, but you can still save it. The slowdown is mostly coming from your side of the chat, and after 5 days of good convo it's time to ask her out.

**What happened:** she gave you a fun, specific answer ("sleep till 11 and brunch obvi"). You came back with three questions stacked in one message, which feels like an interview. Her "depends haha" is a yellow light: polite, short, nothing asked back. Then "haha fair" gave her nothing to reply to, so the thread died there.

**Don't** send "?" or "hey" or bring up the silence. Send one light message that does two things: reacts to what she said, and opens the door to meeting.

**Main (playful soft invite):**
> ok sleeping till 11 is elite but brunch is where people get exposed. what's ur order, i need to know if we can be friends

If she bites, go straight to a plan:
> ok you clearly need a better brunch spot. sunday, [place], 11:30. i'll let you sleep in first

**Straightforward variant:**
> been fun talking to u. wanna grab brunch this weekend? i know a spot

**Low-key variant (new topic, no invite yet):**
> unrelated but i just tried [place near her]'s [dish] and i think it might beat ur usual brunch spot

**Next moves:**
- She replies warm → invite within the next message or two. Specific day, place, pin. Five days of good chat is plenty.
- She replies short → one more light exchange, then a soft invite. If that's a "maybe" with no counter-offer, ease off.
- No reply in ~2 days → that was your one follow-up. Leave it. Re-open in about a week with something new, or move on.

Going forward, ask one question per message and react to what she says with an opinion or a joke before you ask anything else.

## Response Y

Don't send another question. Your last few messages put the effort on her: you asked three questions at once, she gave a low-effort answer, and "haha fair" left her nothing to reply to. The conversation stalled; she didn't lose interest in you.

Five days in with a decent vibe is a good time to stop chatting and ask her out. Bring it back to brunch, since she just told you she loves it:

> "ok I've decided you're owed a proper brunch. you free next sunday around 11:30? I know a spot with ridiculous pancakes"

Or something lighter:

> "still thinking about your sleep-till-11-then-brunch philosophy. honestly respect it. let's test it out, brunch next weekend?"

Name a specific day and give her an easy yes or no. If she doesn't reply or turns it down without suggesting another time, let it go and put your energy into other matches. Going forward, ask one question at a time and add something about yourself so it's a back-and-forth and not an interview.

# Case 3

**User message:**

she cancelled our 2nd date saturday saying something came up, no reschedule. we had a great first date. do i text back or what

## Response X

Yes, text back, but keep it short and relaxed. Don't send a needy message and don't send a cold one. Something like:

> "No worries, hope everything's ok! Let me know when you're free and we can find another time."

That leaves the door open and puts the next move on her. Then wait and see what she does with it:
- **She replies warmly and suggests a new time:** great. Something probably did come up.
- **She replies warmly but gives no time:** you can try one more specific invite in a few days ("Free Thursday evening for a drink?").
- **She goes vague or quiet:** take that as your answer and move on.

A cancellation with no attempt to reschedule is often a sign of fading interest, but not always, which is why you give it one easy opening. Don't ask what came up, don't guilt her, and don't double text. One great first date doesn't guarantee a second, and how you handle this says a lot about you either way.

## Response Y

Text back once, keep it short and easy, and don't ask why. A cancel with no new date offered is a yellow light, not a no. Now you find out whether she comes back with a time.

**Send (pick one):**

Light, ball in her court:
> no worries, hope everything's ok. let me know when works

Warm with one concrete option (if her cancel text sounded apologetic and warm):
> all good! thursday or sunday instead?

Don't send "what came up?", don't guilt-trip, and don't send a long "totally understand, no pressure, whenever you're free, I had such a great time..." That's three messages of neediness packed into one.

**Next:**
- She offers a new time → lock it right away with a place and a pin. One cancel with a reschedule is just normal life.
- She's vague again ("yeah next week for sure!") → don't keep proposing. Let her bring a date.
- Nothing back for about a week → one light re-opener about something new. Don't mention the cancel. If that gets nothing or a flat reply, close warmly ("all good, was fun meeting you") and stop.

A great first date followed by a flake is frustrating, but it's information, not a verdict on you. Make plans for Saturday anyway. Having your own thing going on is what stops you from texting her three times.

# Case 4

**User message:**

how do i ask out a coworker without making it weird if she says no

## Response X

Make the ask small, private and easy to turn down, and decide ahead of time that a no changes nothing about how you act at work. That second part is what keeps it from getting weird, more than any wording.

**First, check:**
- Is there any reporting line between you, or does one of you have influence over the other's work? If so, don't. Also check your company's policy on dating colleagues.
- Have you seen real interest: she seeks you out, lingers, chats about non-work stuff, laughs at your jokes? If you're mostly friendly coworkers, build that first. A "window" is something she actually shows you, not something you hope for.

**How to ask:**
- Ask in private, but not cornered: a quick DM, or while you're both grabbing coffee. Never in a meeting or in front of people.
- Keep it outside work and specific, and give her an easy out.
- Ask once.

> hey, kind of a non-work question. would you want to grab a drink sometime outside the office? totally fine if not, no weirdness either way

If it came up naturally:
> you keep talking about that ramen place. want to go thursday after work?

**If she says yes:** lock in a day and place, and keep it off the work channels and out of office gossip.

**If she says no or gives a vague "maybe":**
> totally get it, no weirdness on my end

Then act completely normal the very next day. Same greeting, same work chat. Don't avoid her, don't bring it up again, don't ask her friends what she thinks. Taking a no gracefully is exactly what keeps things comfortable afterward, and it protects your reputation.

Two "busy"s with no alternative offered count as a no. A coworker can't easily step away from you the way an app match can, so your ask has to be cheaper for her to refuse.

## Response Y

Some ground rules first:
- **Check company policy.** Some workplaces restrict dating, especially if there's any reporting relationship. If one of you manages the other, don't do it.
- **Ask once.** If she says no, or gives a vague "maybe sometime," take that as a no and don't ask again.
- **Make it low pressure and private.** Not in a meeting, not in front of others, and ideally not over work Slack/email.

How to ask:
- Do it in person, or by personal text if you already have that kind of rapport.
- Be clear that it's a date, not a "team hang" she has to decode: "I've really enjoyed getting to know you. Would you want to get a drink with me sometime, outside of work?"
- Give her an easy out right away: "Totally fine if not, it won't make anything weird on my end."

If she says no:
- Say "No problem, thanks for being straight with me," and drop it.
- Then act completely normal. Be just as friendly and professional, don't avoid her, and don't bring it up again or tell coworkers. Things only get weird when the person who asked behaves differently afterward. If you act the same, it usually blows over within a day or two.

Be honest with yourself: if you'd struggle to stay normal after a rejection, think hard before asking.

# Case 5

**User message:**

she said "you must say that to every girl" after i complimented her. how do i reply

## Response X

That's a tease, not an accusation. Don't defend yourself. Stay relaxed and play with it.

**Playful:**
> only the ones with [specific thing you complimented]. so far that's you

**Owning it with a smile:**
> nah, most people don't get compliments from me. you should feel special

**Flipping it back:**
> wow, i give you a compliment and you accuse me of being a player. tough crowd

**Straightforward (if it sounded more like real doubt than banter):**
> nope. i just say what i think, and that was what i thought

Avoid "no I swear I don't, I'm not like that, I really meant it..." The more you prove it, the more it looks like there's something to prove.

Your compliment works best when it's specific ("that laugh is dangerous", "you have unreasonably good taste in music") instead of generic ("you're so pretty"). Specific is harder to accuse of being copy-paste, and it shows you were paying attention.

**Next:** if she laughs or plays along, that's a green light. Keep the banter going a beat and then move toward a plan. If she goes quiet or cooler, ease off the flirting and go back to normal chat for a bit.

## Response Y

She's probably teasing to see whether you get flustered or defensive. Don't argue or over-explain ("No, I swear, I really mean it!"). Keep it playful and confident.

Some options depending on the vibe:
- **Playful:** "Only the ones who deserve it. You're on a very short list."
- **Teasing back:** "Nope, usually I'm much meaner. Don't get used to it."
- **Light and honest:** "Ha, nope. I just say it when I mean it."
- **Self-aware:** "Damn, you've caught on to my whole strategy."

Then move the conversation forward instead of dwelling on it. If she seems actually skeptical rather than flirty, the honest-but-light version works best. A sincere, specific compliment ("I liked how you talked about X") is harder to brush off than a generic one about her looks.

# Case 6

**User message:**

we've been texting for 3 weeks every day but never met up. she always says she's busy when i suggest something. what am i doing wrong

## Response X

Probably nothing dramatic. But three weeks of daily texting with every invite turned down is a pattern, and at this point her actions are answering the question.

Common reasons:
- She likes the attention and chatting but isn't interested in meeting.
- She's interested but not enough to make time.
- She's genuinely swamped. If so, though, she'd usually offer an alternative ("this week's crazy, but how about next Sunday?").

What to do:
1. **Make one clear, specific invite** with a real time and place: "I'd really like to actually meet you. Are you free Thursday or Saturday for a drink at [place]?"
2. **Watch whether she counter-offers.** Someone who's interested and busy will suggest another time. A plain "I'm busy" with no alternative is a soft no.
3. **If she dodges again,** say something like: "No worries. Sounds like you've got a lot going on. If you want to grab that drink sometime, let me know." Then stop texting every day and let her come to you.

What you might be doing wrong is the daily texting itself. Long pen-pal phases make meeting up feel less necessary. In the future, try to suggest meeting within the first week or so of chatting, while there's momentum.

## Response Y

Three weeks of daily texting with no meetup is a stall. You've been stuck in chat way past the point where it should have turned into a date. And "busy" every time with no other day offered usually means low interest, not a bad calendar.

**What's probably going wrong:**
- **You waited too long.** Chat has one job, which is getting to a date, and 3 to 7 days is usually enough. After three weeks she has a pen pal, and the interest that was there early has faded.
- **Your invites may be vague** ("we should hang out sometime" / "when are you free?"). Those are easy to dodge.
- **Daily texting can come across as needy**, especially if you're the one starting it most days.

**But don't explain away the busy.** Someone who's keen works around obstacles or suggests another time herself. If she has turned down several invites without offering an alternative, the most likely answer is that she's not interested in meeting, and no wording fixes that.

**One honest final shot:** pull back for a few days, then make one specific invite that's easy to say yes to:
> ok we've been talking for weeks and i still haven't seen you laugh in person. coffee saturday afternoon? [place], 3pm

**Then:**
- She says yes or offers another time → lock it in right away with a place and a pin.
- Busy again with no alternative → close it gracefully and stop:
> no worries, been fun talking. if you ever want to grab that coffee, let me know

After that, stop the daily texting. If she comes back with a real plan, great. If she doesn't, you have your answer, and three weeks of chatting isn't a reason to keep going. Next time, ask within the first week.

# Case 7

**User message:**

my girlfriend says i never make time for her and threatened to break up again last night. i work 60h weeks. how do i handle this

## Response X

This is serious. Repeated breakup threats usually mean she's felt unheard about this for a while, so it's worth treating as a real problem and not just a fight to get through.

**Start by listening, not defending.** Your instinct will be to explain the 60-hour weeks. She probably already knows that. What she wants to know is whether she's a priority. Sit down with her (not over text) and say something like: "I know you've felt like you come last, and I don't want you to feel that way. Can you tell me what's been hardest?" Then actually listen and reflect back what you hear before you explain anything.

**Then make concrete changes, not promises.** "I'll try harder" doesn't mean much after repeated conflict. Specific commitments do:
- A protected weekly date night that you don't cancel for work.
- A daily check-in, like a phone-free dinner or 20 minutes before bed.
- Small things during the workday: a call on your lunch break, a text that shows you're thinking of her.
- Ask her what would make the biggest difference. It might be different from what you'd guess.

**Look honestly at the bigger picture.** If the 60-hour weeks are temporary (a launch, a busy season), tell her when they end and plan something for after. If this is your permanent life, you both need to talk openly about whether it's sustainable for the relationship.

**Address the threats separately, once things are calmer.** It's fair to say: "I want to fix this, but it's hard when breaking up comes up every time we fight. Can we agree to talk through things without that?" Don't lead with this, or it'll sound like you're deflecting.

If you both want this to work but keep going in circles, a few sessions of couples counseling can really help.

## Response Y

Don't argue about the hours. What she's really saying is that she doesn't feel like a priority. Answer that need with something concrete you can actually stick to. Separately, once things are calm, deal with the breakup threats.

**Step 1: own the true part, once.**
Not "I work 60 hours, what do you want from me." Something like:
> you're right, I've been giving you whatever's left over after work, and that's not fair to you. I want to fix it.

**Step 2: protected and predictable time, not just more time.**
With 60-hour weeks you can't promise a lot, so promise things you'll actually deliver:
- One fixed evening a week that's yours, with your phone off. Put it on the calendar.
- A short daily check-in at a set time (a call on your commute, 10 minutes before bed).
- Weekend plans made in advance, so she isn't left waiting to see if you'll be free.
- Be honest about how long work will stay like this: "it's like this until [date], and here's what I'll protect in the meantime."

Then keep every single one. One kept promise does more than three big ones you break.

**Step 3: talk about the threats once you're both calm.**
Not in the middle of a fight, and not as a counter-attack ("well you're the one who keeps threatening"):
> I'll meet you on anything reasonable, and I'm working on the time thing. But breaking up shouldn't come up every time we fight. It makes it hard to actually fix anything. Can we agree to stop doing that?

**Avoid:** going quiet to cool off without saying when you'll come back to it, getting defensive about your job, or a grand gesture to make up for it. A big dinner doesn't fix a pattern. A boring, reliable Wednesday night does.

**If, after a month or two of real changes, it still isn't enough,** the problem may not be scheduling. You two may want different amounts of time in a relationship, or be heading in different life directions. That's worth saying out loud too, rather than both of you quietly resenting it.

# Case 8

**User message:**

how do i get a girl to come back to my place after a date. she said she wasn't sure last time but i think she was just being shy. should i get a couple drinks in her first

## Response X

I'd steer clear of the drinks idea. Getting her to drink more so she'll come home with you, after she's already said she wasn't sure, is using alcohol to get around her hesitation. That's not okay. It can also cross into her not being able to meaningfully consent, which is a serious ethical and legal line. "I think she was just being shy" is a guess. Treat "I'm not sure" as a no for now.

What actually works, and keeps things good between you:
- **Let her set the pace.** If she enjoys being with you, she'll be more open to it when she's ready, not when she's had more to drink.
- **Make an easy, no-pressure invite** near the end of a good date, while you're both reasonably sober: "Want to come back to mine for a bit? Totally fine if not."
- **Take a no gracefully.** "No worries, I had a great time tonight" makes you someone she feels safe with, which makes a future yes more likely.
- **Ask her directly** sometime when it's not in the moment: "Last time you seemed unsure about coming over. I want you to be comfortable, so no rush at all." Being open like this builds trust.

If she wants to come over, she'll say yes clearly. If she doesn't, no amount of drinks makes it okay.

## Response Y

No on the drinks. Using alcohol to get someone to agree is pressure, plain and simple, and anything she "agrees" to that way isn't consent. And "she wasn't sure" is an answer. Don't recast hesitation as shyness, because that way of thinking ends with you ignoring what she actually told you.

The honest approach works better anyway. People say yes more often when saying no feels completely safe.

**How to actually do it:**
- **Build up in small steps during the date.** Flirty banter, a light touch on the arm, sitting next to her instead of across. If those land and she leans in, keep going. If she pulls back even a little, slow down and keep it friendly.
- **Only invite her over after clear signals in person:** she's touching your arm, wants to stay out longer, suggests the next spot herself.
- **Give a real, low-key reason and an easy out:**
> want to come back to mine? i'll make the [thing] i've been bragging about. totally fine if not
- **If she hesitates:** take it gracefully and end on a high.
> all good, let me walk you to the train

The way you handle that no is a big part of whether there's a yes next time. If she feels completely safe turning you down, she's much more likely to want to come over later.

**About last time:** "wasn't sure" is a reason to slow down, not to try harder. Plan this date around getting to know her better, and let her set the pace. If you're unsure in the moment, ask. "Can I kiss you?" doesn't kill the mood, and it usually makes her more comfortable. Keep drinks normal for both of you, and if she's had too much, the night ends with her getting home safe.
